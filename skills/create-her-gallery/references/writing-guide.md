# Editorial and writing guide

## Begin with evidence

Build chapters from supplied memories, quotations, dates, places, objects, and media. Separate facts from interpretation. Ask when a missing fact would materially change the story.

## Curate the individual

Write about the person, not an abstract ideal of womanhood. Do not reduce her to family roles. Notice agency, humor, contradiction, work, taste, friendship, solitude, and change when the material supports them.

## Make every structure original

Propose chapter structures after reviewing the material. A chronology, a set of places, recurring objects, relationships, seasons, letters, or a single day may each be right. Never copy the chapter taxonomy of 枫、华、or 奕 into a new work.

## Keep poetry concrete

Prefer a remembered bowl, train window, voice message, plant, coat, or habit over generic stars, light, warmth, greatness, and unconditional love. Use metaphor sparingly and consistently. Do not invent events for a prettier line.

## Review before building

Ask the user to confirm names, relationships, chronology, direct quotations, sensitive passages, and the final dedication. A nickname is the default public-facing identifier.
